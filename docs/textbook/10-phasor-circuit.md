# The PFN to hΔB Circuit: A Wired Transform That Turns Out Not to Be One

The second operator through the lab is `phasor_vector_shift`. It matters for two
reasons. Scientifically, it is the fan-shaped body's coordinate transform, the step that
turns a heading into the compass frame in which the fly computes travel direction.
Methodologically, it is the test of whether the ensemble machinery is actually generic
or secretly ring-shaped, because the geometry here is a line of columns, not a circle,
and the observable is a centroid offset, not bump persistence.

## What the wiring says

PFN neurons project to hΔB with consistent column offsets: −3 for PFNd and +2 for PFNv
in the structural histogram. Read naively, that is a fixed coordinate rotation compiled
into the wiring. Drive column 6, and hΔB should respond at column 3 through one arm and
column 8 through the other.

The first version of the lab tested exactly that, and got a result that was
directionally right and quantitatively wrong: realised offsets clustered at −1 to −2,
consistent in sign with the wiring but compressed in magnitude by dendritic
integration, with the hΔB response scaling sub-linearly with PFN drive.

The deeper workup started from the observation that the connectome contains three
dynamical elements the static-offset picture ignores:

- **PFN self-recurrence.** 7,970 synapses within PFNd alone. The shift might be
  generated by dynamics rather than inherited from the projection.
- **hΔB→PFN feedback.** 61 edges. A recurrent loop the offset model treats as absent.
- **hΔB internal recurrence.** Previously modelled as on or off, now swept continuously.

## The ensemble

72 members over `pfndGain × pfnvGain × hdRecur (0 to 2) × hdTonic`, with each member
also run under ablation of the three dynamical elements (`pfnRecur`, `hd2pfn`, and hΔB
recurrence set to zero). The probe drives PFN column 6 and measures where the hΔB
population responds, for each arm separately, and then sweeps the driven column to
check whether the transform is rigid.

## The result

Hypothesis classes: 13 `wired_shift`, 55 `passthrough`, 4 `silent`. The class counts
are not the story. The mechanism attribution among the 13 shifted members is:

| mechanism | members |
|---|---|
| `needs_hdb_recur + hd2pfn_feedback` | 2 |
| `needs_hdb_recur + pfn_recur + hd2pfn_feedback` | 2 |
| `needs_hd2pfn_feedback` | 2 |
| `needs_pfn_recur` | 1 |
| `needs_hdb_recur + pfn_recur` | 1 |
| `needs_pfn_recur + hd2pfn_feedback` | 1 |
| `wired_only` | **0** |

Zero of thirteen shifted members produce the shift from the projection alone. Every one
of them loses it when at least one of the recurrent elements is removed. Three further
findings go with this:

1. **The column sweep is never rigid.** The realised offset changes with which column
   is driven. The transform is warped by position, not a clean linear shift.
2. **PFNv rarely realises its +2.** The PFNv arm has 20 cells against PFNd's 40, and
   that asymmetry, invisible in the offset histogram, means the two phasor arms are not
   equivalent in the dynamics.
3. **The generic runner adds arm coupling.** When the circuit was rerun through the
   spec machinery of Chapter 13, some members' PFNd shift required PFNv co-activity
   (`needs_pfnv_silenced` as a mechanism term), which the hand-written lab had not
   tested. The runner found 11 shifted, 56 passthrough and 5 silent at the same seed,
   again within a member or two of the lab.

## The conclusion changed

I want to state plainly what happened here because it is the kind of thing that gets
lost in a results table. The first-pass interpretation was "PFN→hΔB implements a wired
phase shift, attenuated." That is wrong. The correct statement is "the wiring provides
a *bias*, and recurrence and feedback complete it into a shift." Those are different
mechanism classes, and they make different experimental predictions. A reader of the
connectome alone would have published the wired offset as the circuit's function.

## The ranked experiments

| experiment | separation |
|---|---|
| `amp_scaling` | 0.424 |
| `hd_recur_off` | 0.406 |
| `hd2pfn_off` | 0.316 |
| `measure_pfnd_offset` | 0.314 |
| `pfnv_silenced` | 0.312 |
| `sweep_rigidity` | 0.282 |

`amp_scaling` ranks first because the amplitude asymmetry between the arms is itself
mechanism-dependent. The hΔB→PFN feedback loop, which was only added in the deeper
workup, is already a top-three discriminator: removing it collapses the shift in the
members that depend on it. So the experiment that would settle the mechanism, silencing
the feedback while measuring the offset, is now known and ranked.

## What this circuit teaches about the method

This is the clearest case in the book of structure overstating dynamics. The wiring
says −3. The dynamics say −3 is an upper bound that recurrence partially realises and
position warps. The interesting number is not the offset. It is the gap between the
wired offset and the realised one, because the gap is what tells you the transform is
being computed rather than merely copied.
